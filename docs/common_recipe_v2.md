# Common engineering recipe revision, 22 October 2026

The first two 5,000-update runs learned, but retained substantial errors. Their complete VAL8 measurements are released in [the development record](../reports/development_v1_20261022/readout.txt), including all eight cases and both evaluation timepoints. C reached Dice 73.8430%; the full soft-A platform bridge reached 69.5451%. These models trained on different hardware and software; their difference is not an estimate of the GTA effect. Only two of the seven planned v1 models completed. The remaining v1 jobs were closed before starting, with completed models and their original records retained.

A fixed TRAIN-only diagnostic also found input-size sensitivity. On six centerline-centered patches from three training cases, scoring the same central 96-cube support gave C/A case-macro Dice 89.756%/84.850% with 96-cube inputs and 86.690%/83.435% with 128-cube inputs. Two CT-selected patches had empty central support; false-positive voxel counts increased with the larger input. Switching only BatchNorm to current-batch statistics did not materially repair overall errors. This small diagnostic does not establish a BatchNorm cause or show that larger-patch retraining will improve validation performance.

The bounded v2 revision originally declared seven models and made these common changes:

- Train with 128-cube inputs, matching the existing inference size; retain batch size two and the 1 mm cache.
- Start every model from the same fresh random seed, with 10,000 successful optimizer updates, AdamW initial learning rate 0.0003, weight decay 0.0001, polynomial exponent 0.9 and gradient clipping 12. Evaluate all eight validation cases at updates 5,000 and 10,000; select the checkpoint with greatest mean validation Dice.
- Use Python 3.8.10, PyTorch 1.11.0+cu113, NumPy 1.22.4, SciPy 1.10.1, scikit-image 0.21.0 and SimpleITK 2.3.1 on both training roles. Hardware still differs, so the second role retains a full-A bridge. That bridge is not another random seed.

All 24 frozen model, data, geometry, training and historical source files retain their exact bytes. Attribute definitions, supervision rules, loss weights, hard and soft condition arithmetic, operators, normalization, sampling and augmentation algorithms, foreground-sampling probability, split, cache and seed are unchanged. Realized training patches differ: 96-to-128 changes foreground-center jitter from +/-24 to +/-32 voxels, uniform-start bounds, boundary clipping, input tensors and attribute target support/compositions. Equal seeds do not make cross-version samples or voxel-wise augmentations identical; input pairing applies within a matched recipe. Inference remains 128-cube, batch two, overlap 0.5, threshold 0.5, native-grid restoration and no postprocessing. All final models will be evaluated on one reference environment and device.

This is a combined engineering revision. The nominal input-voxel budget is `(128/96)^3 * (10000/5000) = 4.74074` times v1, and changing patch extent also changes patch-composition targets. A v1-to-v2 difference cannot isolate context size, training duration or software effects. The revision is not evidence of GTA superiority and does not promise a particular score.

Both roles passed synthetic operator and gradient checks and a separate real-TRAIN preflight: seven role-specific models each completed two successful updates, with no AMP retries and approximately 12.52–13.03 GiB peak allocated GPU memory. The two real batches had identical input and target hashes across roles within the common 128-cube v2 recipe; they were not a replay of v1 training inputs. Preflight models were discarded without checkpoints or held-out-array access. These are implementation checks, not segmentation results. The legacy CUDA runtime reports unavailable deterministic backward implementations for some operators in warning mode; identical seeds and data do not imply bitwise deterministic training.

V2 retains one frozen 36/8/12 case-level split and one seed. The original seven-job plan was administratively closed after MAIN C and SECOND full-A bridge each completed 10,000 updates and two full VAL8 endpoints; the other five original jobs did not start. The original plan remains an incomplete historical record. After those endpoints and the fixed same-weight deployment diagnostic were inspected, the [current scope was reduced to four model tuples](minimal4_scope_20261022.md): original MAIN C, fresh MAIN A, original SECOND A bridge, and fresh SECOND constant-A. The two new recipes are byte-identical copies of the original v2 A/constant recipes; the original two endpoints retain their original v2 identity and are not relabeled as new training. This is a scope revision, not algorithm v3 or a new training recipe.

The two planned within-role contrasts answer different questions. MAIN A-C measures specialized operations and predicted conditioning jointly, against ordinary attribute-feature fusion with the same auxiliary supervision. SECOND A-constant measures overall predicted conditions, including empty-class mass and changed gradient paths. Without MAIN constant, a MAIN gain cannot be decomposed into condition value. The two A models are platform bridges with the same seed, not additional seeds, and do not support pooling or subtracting a platform effect. Matching core software does not remove hardware effects. Patch-level global composition does not itself establish local anatomical targeting.

The [56 released scan IDs do not establish 56 independent patients](avt_case_independence.md); the cited RIDER test-retest source does not demonstrate an AVT repeat pair or cross-split overlap. No external architecture is trained. MAIN constant, single-condition models and the hard anchor are deferred rather than silently counted as complete.

[Stage results](../reports/development_v2_endpoints_20261022/readout.md) include all 32 original first-two-job case/timepoint metrics and four summaries. Overall Dice improved, but both models' fine-radius group recall regressed relative to their own v1 endpoints. [The fixed V1 same-weight deployment diagnostic](../reports/development_extent_v1_20261022/readout.md) completed all four conditions and three cases: 96 increased recall with substantial FP/centerline-precision costs and did not support a common rollback. These are development evidence after which scope changed, not a fair GTA or final TEST conclusion.

After all four model selections are fixed, the plan uses one common evaluation environment/device, plus a validation-only availability-preserving intervention on the same SECOND A weights, without retuning. Only a separate explicit action after all-four selection freeze may evaluate TEST12. The historical three-MAIN-classifier attribute protocol requires a MAIN constant model that the reduced scope lacks; it has not been completed and cannot accept cross-role substitutions. Full recipes remain in evaluation provenance; a scope label must not bypass source, role/job, recipe or checkpoint identity. No test or larger phase starts automatically.
